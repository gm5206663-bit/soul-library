# Chapter 6 — The Hem Road

The hem road was not a road. It was what was left when forest decided not to be forest for a while, a strip of hard ground between trees and tilled ground, beaten by feet and cart rims and weather, grass growing up its middle and beaten down again. Villages knew it. Hunters knew it. No one owned it. That was why his father had said hem, and north, and never in trees.

He walked it north.

First morning he learned its arithmetic. Dawn was cold and thin, and the ledger was simply there, like floor. He looked and found it honest. Thirty. Two rings aging in seats, purple thousand-year, concealed as yellow to world via Ring Veil. Three bloodlines, three slots, Grey Ridge Hunt in third. He did not write it. There was no update and no just gain, and the rule for this road was the rule the author had given: only write full when there is update or just gain, normally check status file. So he checked it in mind and walked.

He called grey on him for whole mornings now. Silent *hunt.* Grey light surged from within, faint ice-amber light fluctuating, bones cracking, muscles and bones expanding, stature larger, hair grey with frost tint, amber eyes ice-amber, hands claws, nails sharp like daggers, grey fur faint on arms, cold air exhaled, wind curling around feet. Beast possession. Attributes superimposed upon body. Changes pronounced as cultivation rose, fusion degree affecting how completely strength expressed, closer to Storm Frost Ghost Wolf characteristics better unleash power, body robust released or not. That was possession, canon's own base beast law, and at eleven it held whole morning and asked for more.

Frame had arrived as freight across a season. That freight now walked. Amber in eyes caught firelight, grey tint in hair when sun hit it. Village dogs stopped lifting heads when he passed. Wood passed him at peace when he sat bracken in possession. That was new. That was Grey High body-line, vitality, recovery, predator's frame, denser bone, quicker muscle, reflexes step ahead, body slowly becoming wolf's released or not. With two thousand-year rings, pool deepened and pathways widened and hardened, and the body could lift near five hundred kilos at eleven, three times normal boy, not by straining but by being built different. Mother's coat, rebuilt as road coat with pelts sewed into hem, weighted when lifted, and he knew better than to count it in front of her.

The hem road taught Mountain Stride in a different alphabet. It was not ridge trails following. It was flat, and flat has its own lies. Feet want to fall same way each time, and same way is how blisters come. He had learned at nine that Mountain Stride was not walking faster. It was walking so ground did not know you had passed. At mastery now, it was limb. He could walk whole day and at evening his feet did not know they had walked.
