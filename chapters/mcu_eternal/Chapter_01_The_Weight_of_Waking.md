# Chapter One — The Weight of Waking

```
◆ STATUS — Chapter One: FORGE (pre-Earth, before 5000 BC)
KIND      : Eternal, Eleventh, freshly activated
AGE       : newborn on this mission; prior missions wiped
CLASS     : Order I Foundational, Nascent — first flexes only
THREAT    : the mountain's gaze (Arishem's examination)
LAW       : the mission law is spoken — "we watch"
```

---

He woke to falling that wasn't falling.

There was no ground to strike, no air to catch him — only a long, slow pull through warm dark, as if the universe had him by the chest and was deciding where to set him down. His fingers spread. His knees drew up. His body braced for an impact his mind had no memory of ever surviving.

The impact never came.

Instead: stillness. A cradle of light under his back. A hum beneath hearing that lived in his teeth.

Varun opened his eyes — though he did not yet know the name, did not yet know he was someone who could be named — and saw gold.

---

Thin lines of it crawled over his hands. Circles inside circles, joined by hairline seams, brightening and dimming with a rhythm that matched nothing until, gradually, it matched his breathing. He watched his own fingers curl. The lattice bent with the skin and did not break. It was under the skin, he understood. It was the skin, and under it, and deeper than that, all the way down to whatever he was built on.

*Built.* The word arrived with the certainty of an imprint. *You were built. You are Eternal. You are the Eleventh.*

He knew he was from Olympia. It sat in his mind like a stone in clear water — smooth, certain, false.

His hands did not believe it.

That was his first thought that was truly his: his hands, turning in the light, shaping themselves around a weight that wasn't there, moving with a care his minutes-old mind had never taught them. As if they had held something before. As if they had *caught* something before, and remembered the shape of catching the way a shore remembers a wave.

A chime sounded. The cradle tilted. The warm dark thinned —

— and the Forge took him.

---

There are no words in any tongue of Earth for the World-Forge, because no tongue of Earth was ever meant to speak inside it. Varun would spend seven thousand years trying, in a hundred languages, and fail in all of them. What he remembered, always, was *scale*: vaults of dark metal going up until up stopped meaning anything, rivers of molten gold light running in channels wide as seas, and silence — a working silence, full of motion, full of beings too large to notice him the way a man does not notice the dust on his own sleeve.

Ten other lights burned in cradles around his, arranged in an arc. Ten others waking, or woken. He felt them the way he felt his own heartbeat — distant, certain, *his*, though he had no word yet for brothers, for sisters, for team.

The hum in his teeth deepened. The gold rivers slowed.

Something was looking at him.

It did not arrive. It did not appear. One moment the vault above was empty dark; the next, attention rested on him like a mountain rests on the earth — not cruel, not kind, simply *total*. His cradle groaned. His fresh-forged bones sang with strain. Breath stopped being automatic and became a problem to solve, each inhalation hauled up against a pressure that wanted him flat, wanted him still, wanted him *known*.

His knees bent. His forehead went down. He did not decide to kneel; the weight decided, and his body agreed before his mind could object. That agreement — that unthinking, whole-frame yielding that still kept him *upright inside himself* — was the first thing his existence ever learned to do, and he would do it again, years beyond counting, on a colder world, under a smaller sky, and remember this moment and understand it had been practice.

**ELEVENTH.**

The word was not sound. It was weather.

**GRAVITY-ANCHOR. KINETIC-SHUNT. EARTH-MISSION.**

Each designation landed like a stone dropped into deep water, and each one sank into him and stayed. He felt them settle: one in his hands, one in his feet, one behind his eyes. Function. Place. Purpose. The weight leaned closer, examining —

— and found nothing wrong, and moved on.

The mountain lifted. Air rushed back into him. Varun stayed kneeling in his cradle with his forehead nearly touching his knees and his fingers dug into the light, breathing like a man hauled from the sea, alive in a way he had not been moments before. Because now he knew the most important thing anyone had ever taught him, though no one had spoken it:

*I am. Something else is. The something else is heavier than me, and I am still here.*

He would forget many things. The Forge would see to that, in time. He would never forget the shape of that knowing. It lived in his knees, in his breath, in the exact angle his spine had found to endure without breaking.

Footsteps. Small ones — human-scale, after the mountain. A hand, warm and calloused, settled on his shoulder.

"Breathe with me," said a woman's voice. "In. Out. Slower. Good. He does that to everyone first. It isn't punishment. It's how He reads."

Varun raised his head.

---

She was not large. That was his first surprise — after the mountain, everything would be small, but she was small the way a drawn bow is small: held, ready, kind on purpose rather than by accident. Healer's hands. Commander's eyes. Gold at her cuffs and throat, catching the Forge-light.

"I am Ajak," she said. "Prime Eternal of the Earth mission. Which makes you mine." A pause, and something almost like humor moved across her face. "He gave you function. I give you courtesies. On Earth they will call us gods whether we ask them to or not, so you need a name that won't embarrass you in a temple." She studied him — the way his fingers still shaped themselves around absent weight, the way his feet had already found the cradle's balance point without looking. "Depth. Holding. The sea that keeps ships and drowns them. *Varun.*"

"Varun," he repeated. The name fit the way the kneel had fit — as if somewhere under the newness, a shape had been waiting for it.

"Come," Ajak said. "Meet the ten who will be your everything."

---

They came to him in impressions, because his mind was minutes old and could hold nothing larger.

*Heat and certainty* — a man with a jaw like a prow, who looked at Varun the way one measures a wall: how high, how thick, will it hold. "Eleventh," said Ikaris, and nodded once, and that was either welcome or dismissal, and Varun would spend centuries deciding which.

*Edge* — a woman whose stillness was a drawn blade, gold spear-light flickering at her fingertips and gone. Thena inclined her head. "Don't stand behind me in a fight until you know why," she said. It was the kindest warning he received that day.

*Warmth* — vast hands, a laugh like a landslide, the smell of something cooking somewhere impossible. "You're thin, little anchor," said Gilgamesh cheerfully. "We'll fix that. Everything fights better fed." He pressed something into Varun's hand — bread, still warm — and Varun, who had never eaten, ate, and understood *comfort* before he understood *hunger*.

*Water over stones* — gentle eyes, a touch light on his forearm to steady him when the cradle's hum made his knees dip. "Easy," Sersi smiled. "The first hour is the worst hour. It gets—" she glanced at the vault above, at the memory of the mountain, "—different."

*Laughter* — "Eleven! An odd number! I love it, the symmetry was suffocating me!" Kingo spun a coin of solid light across his knuckles and vanished it. "When the temples get built — and they will, trust me — ask for me by name. I give excellent blessings."

*A tug on his sleeve and a wink* — a child with eyes far older than her face. "You're going to be fun to lie to," Sprite announced. "You believe with your whole face." She was gone before he could answer, and his sleeve was tied in a knot he hadn't felt her tie.

*Scanning light and fast fingers* — a man circled him with a device that hummed against Varun's lattice, frowning at readings only he could see. Phastos glanced up. "Hmm. Routing's clean, but there's — here, in the motor pathways — residual shaping. Pre-imprint. Like hands that practiced before the mind woke." He tapped the device. "Drift's within tolerance. Flagging it anyway." He caught Varun staring. "Don't worry. Everybody's got something."

*Rhythm without sound* — she didn't walk up so much as *arrive*, all at once, vibration preceding her like the bow-wave of a ship. Makkari studied him for three full seconds, head tilted, then touched two fingers to her own chest, then to his sternum, then pointed at the floor and raised her eyebrows: *feel that?* The Forge's hum through the deck. He nodded, and she grinned — quick and bright — and was elsewhere, and the vibration of her passing stayed in his ribs like a struck bell.

And last —

*Cold.* A young man with ancient eyes stopped a careful distance away and looked at Varun the way the mountain had looked, but smaller, and deliberate, and *inward* — as if reaching. For one heartbeat Varun felt pressure behind his eyes, a fingertip touching the surface of his thoughts —

— and his knees remembered the kneel, his breath remembered the rhythm, his spine remembered the angle, and the fingertip found no purchase and withdrew.

Druig smiled thinly. "Interesting," he said, and walked away.

Ajak had watched all of it. She watched a moment longer, at the place where Varun's hands had curled — unconsciously, protectively — at his sides. Something moved behind the commander's eyes: recognition, or suspicion, or grief preemptively felt. Then it was gone.

"Phastos," she said evenly. "Is he fit?"

"Fit." A pause. "Deep, for a fresh build. But fit."

"Good." Ajak turned to Varun. "Rest. *Breathe.* The Domo leaves for Earth when the Forge releases us, and Earth — " for the first time, something softened in her voice, something the commander allowed the believer, "— Earth is worth every mile."

---

The tool floated.

Varun didn't mean it. He was sitting on the cradle's edge, practicing *standing* — the deck was steady, his frame was strong, but strength kept arriving slightly before balance, and twice he'd had to catch himself on the cradle rail with a clang that echoed. Phastos's dropped calibrator drifted past his shoulder on its way to the floor, and his hand came up without instruction, fingers shaping around absent weight the way they'd shaped since waking —

— and the calibrator stopped falling.

It hung in the air beside his palm, turning slowly, gold lattice blooming across the back of his hand like frost on a window. Varun stared at it. The calibrator stared back, in the manner of small inanimate things. His breath caught — and the lattice flickered — and the calibrator dropped the last inch into his palm with a small, ordinary *tok*.

Silence in the bay.

"Well," said Kingo brightly, from across the cradle arc. "That's going to be *extremely* annoying in dice games."

Laughter broke the freeze — Gilgamesh's landslide, Sersi's soft exhale, even a sound from Thena that might have been amusement. Phastos crossed the bay in four strides, took the calibrator, scanned Varun's hand, scanned it again.

"Field coherence at first flex," he muttered. "No training, no primer, and he feathers the edge instead of grabbing. Do you know how long feathering takes to learn?" He looked up. "You don't. Because you were born an hour ago."

"Drift," said Ajak quietly. She wasn't looking at the device. She was looking at Varun's hands — at the way they had already, unconsciously, shaped themselves for the next catch. "Within tolerance?"

Phastos held her gaze a beat too long. "...Within tolerance," he said, and logged it, and the log would sit in the Domo's memory for seven thousand years, unread and accruing interest.

Above them, high in the vault where the gold rivers ran, something shifted — a service gantry realigning, a shard of cradle-glass breaking loose from the works. It fell silently through the vast air, turning end over end, catching light, aimed by nothing and no one at the exact crown of Varun's unprotected head.

His hands moved before the warning shout finished leaving Sersi's throat.

Later, no one could agree on what they'd seen. Kingo swore the shard *slowed* first, the air going thick around it. Makkari signed that she'd felt the deck *lean*, a tilt that corrected itself. Phastos's device recorded a spike — kinetic bloom, his word for it, coined on the spot — and a gold flash, and then: Varun standing with the shard buried half an inch in his raised palm, blood welling bright and strange and red, staring at his own hand with an expression no one present had the experience to name.

It was *recognition*. Not of the shard. Of the sting. Of the exact *cost* of catching something too fast to catch.

His palm sealed itself before Ajak reached him. The blood remained, bright on gold lattice, until he wiped it on his suit and left a smear that the suit drank slowly, thoughtfully, as if tasting him.

"Well caught, little anchor," Gilgamesh said gently, into the quiet. "Next time, step aside."

Varun nodded. His hand throbbed pleasantly. His feet had found the stance without being taught — one forward, one back, weight sunk low. The stance of someone who intended, against all available evidence, to *hold*.

---

The briefing was short. Celestial briefings always were; eternity does not elaborate.

Ajak stood before the eleven with Earth's image turning above her palm — blue and white and impossibly small — and gave them the law that would shape seven thousand years:

"The Deviants were sent to cull the predators. They turned on the shepherds instead. We are sent to finish them." The image turned. Continents drifted. "Where Deviants threaten humans, we act. Where humans threaten humans — " her voice did not change, and that was how Varun knew the words cost her, though he was an hour old and knew nothing of cost, "— we do not interfere. We guide. We guard the mission. We do not choose their wars."

Eleven lights. Eleven silences.

Varun heard his own voice before he knew he intended to speak. It came out rough, unused, honest:

"If they are dying — and it is not Deviants — do we watch?"

The silence changed shape. Ikaris's approval was a physical warmth at his back: *good soldier, seeking clarity.* Druig's smirk was a cold thread: *oh, this one asks.* Sersi looked away — quickly, but Varun's new eyes were Eternal eyes, and they caught it: the flinch, the already-grief, the question she had asked herself and buried.

Ajak met his gaze. Held it. And in her face he saw the whole terrible architecture of command — the knowing and the carrying, the truth behind the truth she would spend millennia deciding whether to share.

"Yes," she said. "We watch."

The word landed in him like the designations had — a stone into deep water. But this one did not settle. This one *sank*, down past the newness, down to wherever his hands kept their unbelonging memories, and sat there, unsinking-further, waiting.

*We watch.*

"Board the Domo," Ajak said.

---

Earth filled the viewport the way the mountain had filled the vault — total, unhurried, *there*. Blue deepened into blue. White curled and tore. Somewhere under those clouds, things with teeth were hunting things with hands, and eleven lights were falling toward them with orders to kill the teeth and never touch the hands' wars.

Varun stood at the glass with his palm pressed flat against it, feeling the ship's hum through the deck, through his boots, through the bones of his feet — Makkari's lesson, learned in a glance: *feel that?* He felt it. He felt the Domo's weight and his own weight and, faint as a rumor, the pull of the world below reaching up to take him.

*Falling that wasn't falling.* That was how he'd woken. That was how he'd go down.

Behind him, the ten moved through departure drills — Ikaris calling intervals, Thena checking weapons that were part of her, Gilgamesh laughing at something Kingo said, Sersi quiet at another window. Phastos arguing softly with his own instruments, Sprite's mischief a bright thread, Makkari's vibration everywhere at once, Druig's cold a shadow at the edge, Ajak's command a steady hand over all of it.

His. *His*, the imprint said. *Your everything.*

Varun watched the blue grow, and his hands shaped themselves around absent weight, and the unsunk stone of *we watch* sat in the deep of him, waiting for seven thousand years of evidence.

The Domo fell toward morning.

*End of Chapter One.*
